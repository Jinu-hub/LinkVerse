The four-week challenge is over.

When I started, I didn't have a clear picture of what it would become.

I wanted to build something with an AI Agent, and I already had news collection and report automation pipelines underway.

But producing data and making that data easy for people to actually use are completely different problems.

That was the point I kept coming back to over these four weeks.

**How should I present all this information I can already generate?**

And what took shape from that question is the **DAMI** I have now.

---

## There Is Already Enough Information

These days, the harder problem is rarely finding information. More often, there is simply too much of it to know what to look at.

Even if you only try to follow the news lightly, countless articles and reports show up every day.

In markets alone, stories about the macroeconomy, interest rates, stocks, crypto, companies, and policy keep pouring in. In AI, new models, services, papers, and product launches follow one after another every day.

Of course, if you set your mind to it, you could read all of it.

But the problem is time.

And more precisely, it is the question of **whether I need to see all of it at all**.

I look at a fair amount of market and AI information every day myself, but what I actually want to know comes down to a few things.

What matters today.

Whether anything has changed around the topics I care about.

And whether I can go a little deeper when something truly sparks my curiosity.

DAMI started from those questions.

---

## At First, It Was About Making a Briefing

The first idea was simple.

If I gathered the day's main issues, summarized them briefly, and turned them into a briefing you could read in about 30 seconds, that would be quite convenient.

Add voice, and you could listen on the commute.

If you got more curious, you could open the full report.

Up to that point, the flow felt fairly natural.

But as I built it, one problem became clear.

**Showing the same briefing to everyone was not enough.**

Some people care about interest rates.  
Some care about AI infrastructure.  
Some care about Bitcoin or a specific company.

Even when reading the same report, what feels important differs from person to person.

So after the briefing,  
wouldn't it be better to surface only the stories the user actually cares about?

That is where the current **For You** and interest features began.

---

## Personalization That Starts with a Single ★

In DAMI, you can add a ★ to a topic or keyword.

It is a very simple feature.

But in the direction I have in mind for DAMI, it is also a fairly important one.

Based on the topics a user marks as interesting,  
it finds the related parts inside a long report and brings them forward.

You do not need to read the full report from start to finish.

You can see first what is relevant to you today,  
and go deeper only when you need to.

Personalization is still simple at this stage,  
but I think this small star could become a starting point for how DAMI grows.

If it connects to what users look at often,  
which topics they keep following,  
and what kinds of questions they ask lately,

then someday it may go beyond simply showing the keywords they chose —

and grow into a service that can offer first,

**“Today, this might be worth a look.”**

---

## There Does Not Have to Be Only One Way to Read

While building DAMI, another thing I kept thinking about  
was that there does not have to be only one way to deliver information.

The same data can be

seen as a 30-second briefing,

heard as audio,

filtered down to only the parts you care about,

read as a full report,

or asked about again in chat.

In the past, I thought the important thing was making one good report.

Creating a solid original report still matters.

But now I also feel that the **way it is expressed** afterward matters just as much.

Creating good data  
and creating a good user experience are different problems.

These four weeks were about trying to connect the two.

---

## The Name DAMI

It took longer than I expected to settle on a service name.

The name I ended up choosing was **DAMI**.

It came from the Korean word *damda* — to hold, or to put something into something.

Rather than a service that shows every piece of information in the world,  
I liked the image of **a small space that holds the stories I care about**.

Even if DAMI gains more features later,  
I want to keep at least that basic feeling.

Not too complicated.

Easy enough to open once a day without pressure.

Fine to glance at and leave.

Able to go a little deeper when something makes you curious.

That is roughly the DAMI I have in mind right now.

---

## Beyond Markets and AI

Right now, DAMI is centered on global markets and AI.

Those are the areas I look at often myself, and I already have automation pipelines running for them.

But finishing this challenge left me thinking that I should also try making content people are genuinely interested in — **not only the topics I personally like**.

It does not have to be difficult or highly specialized information.

I want to try more popular subjects like sports or entertainment as well, and see what people pay attention to and what kinds of information they want more of.

At its core, DAMI is not about a specific topic.

I think it is about

**first organizing what matters out of a lot of information,  
then picking out what is useful to me,  
and making it easy to ask more when something sparks curiosity**.

If that structure works well, the range of stories DAMI can hold can become much wider.

---

## After Four Weeks

Not everything was finished through this challenge.

If anything, now that it exists, I can see even more that still needs work.

Personalization is still simple,  
and I still need to check how quickly a new user can understand DAMI's value on first contact.

The length of the briefing and the density of information will also need ongoing adjustment.

Still, one thing became a little clearer over these four weeks.

What I want to build  
is not a service that gathers a lot of news and shows it.

**It is a service that helps you not miss what you need,  
even if you look at less information overall.**

And when you need to, it lets you take one more step deeper into that information.

Connecting the 30-second briefing and voice,  
For You based on interests,  
and the Full Report with chat  
was ultimately part of finding that direction.

The challenge ends here.

But DAMI is only now starting  
to reveal, little by little, the kind of service I want to build.

For now, it is still a small personal project.

Even so, for a while I want to keep putting  
the stories I want to see, one by one, into this small space.
