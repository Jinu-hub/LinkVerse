## Results That Did Not Match Expectations

This time, I spent longer thinking than before.

I prepared carefully in my own way — from the service structure to the content, the screens, and multilingual support —  
and released Market Memory in a form people could actually use.  
To avoid stopping at development alone, I also created SNS accounts and tried posting in several ways.

But meaningful user traffic never really appeared.

The result was much quieter than I had expected.  
Given the time and hope I had put into building the service, it weighed on me,  
and for a few days it was hard to focus on anything else.

Through that process, one thing became clear.

Trying hard to build a good service, and having people actually find and use it, are entirely different problems.  
Being good at development alone was not enough.

## I Decided to Change the Plan

At first, after finishing Phase 1, I planned to keep expanding features through Phase 2 and Phase 3.

But Phase 2 is not a stage for adding features alone.  
It is a stage where you check real user responses, take feedback, and adjust the direction of the service.

I decided that, with no users, continuing to add features I imagined myself would not mean much.  
Rather than improving the service, it would likely mean expanding an unvalidated hypothesis on my own.

So I decided to stop the original plan here.

Market Memory Phase 1 ends in its current state.

## Not a Shutdown, but Minimal Operation

I do not intend to shut Market Memory down completely.

With the automation and report generation structure already in place, I can keep the service running without much burden.  
New feature development will pause for now, but market reports and core data will keep accumulating.

Over time, the data being collected now may become a foundation for starting development again.  
Experience and new perspectives from other projects may also find their way back into Market Memory.

So this choice is not a declaration of failure, or a decision to abandon the project.

It is closer to keeping things at a minimum for now — without expanding further — until there is a reason and a direction to move again.

## Changing the Starting Point

Until now, I moved by first shaping an idea I came up with,  
building the service, and then showing it to people.

From here, I want to reverse that order.

Rather than deciding what to build first, I want to start by looking at what people are actually struggling with.  
What problems keep showing up,  
and where people are spending time and money to get something done — I want to observe those things, then develop ideas from there.

Of course, I do not know yet whether this is the right answer.

But through this experience, I learned that looking only at what I want to build has clear limits.  
Next time, I want to put observing a little ahead of building when I begin.

Market Memory will slow down for a while.

And I will continue with other study and projects, while practicing looking again at the problems people face.
