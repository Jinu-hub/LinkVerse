## The Reality After Phase 1

I shipped the Market Memory beta,  
and most of the features I wanted for Phase 1 are now in place.

Mobile optimization and smaller refinements are still left,  
but I felt I had at least reached a point where I could show the service outside and start accepting users.

So the next thing I started was marketing.

Compared with development, it is not an area I am familiar with, and not one I can claim to be good at.  
Rather than starting big, I wanted to keep going calmly within a range I could handle.

The first channel I chose was Threads.

I decided that separate accounts by language would be better than mixing English, Japanese, and Korean users in one place,  
so I created three sub-accounts.

On each account, I post the daily market memory in that language.  
When individual reports are generated, I also summarize them briefly and post those too.

On weekdays, each account can end up with five or six posts a day.

It has now been more than a week since I started running them.

Honestly, the results are not good.

There is almost no response,  
and so far not a single user has signed up for Market Memory through Threads.

## Building and Spreading Turned Out to Be Separate Skills

What I felt most sharply through this is that  
the ability to make something and the ability to tell people about it are completely different.

As a developer, I am used to thinking about the features that are needed,  
designing the structure,  
and building something that actually works.

If there is a problem, I can find the cause and fix it.  
If a feature is missing, I can add it.  
If a screen feels awkward, I can improve it.

Marketing was a little different.

Even if I spend time writing posts and publish them steadily,  
there may be no response at all.

It is also hard to tell right away what is going wrong.

Is the post content the problem,  
is it the lack of trust in the account,  
is the service explanation not enough,  
or is Threads simply the wrong channel? Even that is unclear.

A week is still too short to judge the service itself.

At the same time, I also cannot be sure that just posting more in the same way  
will somehow change the result someday.

What I have built so far  
is closer to a pipeline that can distribute good content in multiple languages.

But publishing content,  
and getting people to discover it, care about it, and start using the service,  
turned out to be a different problem.

## Should I Have Thought About Acquisition Earlier?

In this situation,  
I also start to wonder whether I should have thought more carefully from the first planning stage about how to bring people in.

While building Market Memory,  
I spent a lot of time on the report generation structure, multilingual support, the archive, the dashboard, and automation.

On the other hand, where users first discover this service,  
what they see that makes them interested,  
and why they should sign up  
were things I only started thinking about more seriously relatively late.

That does not mean I think the development so far was a mistake.

Market Memory is actually working,  
and the analytical structure I wanted to build is in place to some extent.

Phase 1 of the product may be done,  
but the process of delivering that product to users has only just begun.

Product development has reached Phase 1,  
but user acquisition and marketing may still be at Phase 0.

## The Next Step That Stopped Moving

According to the original plan, I wanted a few users to start signing up by now,  
and to improve the service based on how they used it and what they said.

Along the way, I also planned to make Phase 2 features more concrete  
and gradually organize the roadmap ahead.

But that flow is not forming right now.

Without users, I cannot tell what feels inconvenient in real use.

I also cannot tell what features people want.

And I have no way to check whether the features I thought were important are important to users as well.

If I start adding features again in that state,  
there is a good chance I will just keep building alone what I think is needed.

So right now, it is hard to simply begin the Phase 2 I had already imagined.

That does not mean I want to stop the project.

If anything, I may need to change what Phase 2 means for a while.

Not a stage for expanding features,  
but a stage for directly checking who Market Memory can help, and what value it can give them.

## What to Confirm Before Sign-ups

At first, I thought I needed more sign-ups before I could get feedback.

But if no one comes in on their own,  
waiting alone will not confirm anything.

I need to show it directly to a small number of people first and listen to their reactions.

Before asking whether they signed up,  
there are more basic questions I need to check.

Can someone seeing Market Memory for the first time understand what the service does?

Does a sample report make them want to read more?

Compared with existing news, investing communities, or brokerage apps, is there a reason to come back?

And is the value clear enough that someone would create an account?

Right now, a few users who can answer these questions honestly  
may matter more than dozens of sign-ups.

## I Decided Not to Look Only at Threads

I am not planning to give up on Threads right away.

The accounts are still new, and there is almost no accumulated relationship or trust yet.

Still, it has become clear that the current approach should not become Market Memory's entire marketing strategy.

Increasing the number of posts did not necessarily lead to more interest.

Just steadily publishing automatically generated summaries  
may not be enough to make people remember the account or care about the service.

Going forward, I will need more than stacking posts.  
Joining conversations with related people and exchanging opinions directly also seems necessary.

And next week, I plan to start putting a foot into communities beyond Threads as well.

I am looking at spaces like Reddit and Hacker News,  
where new projects can be introduced and feedback can be received.

I want to avoid scattering links across many places from the start.

I want to look first at what kind of conversations each community has,  
join topics that fit Market Memory, and introduce the project more naturally from there.

Rather than trying to sell,  
it feels more fitting at this stage to speak honestly about why I made this service,  
what problem I am trying to solve,  
and what I want to confirm right now.

## Accepting the Results So Far

The marketing results so far are not good.

I spent time making posts, but there was almost no response,  
and it did not lead to sign-ups either.

There is no need to force a positive reading of that fact.

That does not mean Market Memory as a whole has no value.

What has been confirmed for now  
is that the current Threads approach alone did not bring in users.

It could be a product problem, or a messaging problem.

It could be a channel problem,  
or the problem of expecting results too quickly from accounts that almost no one knows yet.

For now, those still need to be separated and checked one by one.

When I was developing, finished features remained as results.

But after putting a product into the world,  
it seems I also have to accept periods of no response as a kind of result.

Finding where things are stuck inside that silence,  
and trying again in a different way, is also part of building a product.

Right now, I cannot move on to the Phase 2 I originally planned.

Instead, reality is showing me a new Phase 2.

Not a stage for making more features,  
but a stage for finding people, listening to them,  
and confirming why Market Memory is actually needed.

There is still no answer.

But next week, I want to step a little away from repeating the same kind of posting,  
and start talking with people directly in other spaces.

For now, that kind of next step is enough.
