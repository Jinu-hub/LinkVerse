## The Week After Deployment

Last week, the Market Memory beta was successfully deployed to Vercel.

There are still fine adjustments to make, but the main features that needed to be in place for the MVP have largely taken shape.  
I am now moving, little by little, from a “building” phase toward a phase of refining with operations in mind.

This week, rather than adding major new features,  
I worked through the smaller adjustments that become necessary once the service is actually deployed and viewed as a live product.

## Improvements Made

The work this week can be grouped into a few areas.

First, I made small UI refinements.  
Things worked functionally, but some parts still felt awkward on screen, and I adjusted those one by one.

I also worked on mobile optimization.  
Market Memory is strongly oriented toward reading reports on desktop,  
but users are likely to visit from mobile as well.  
In particular, I felt the dashboard and report list needed at least a minimum level of readability on mobile.

I adjusted report titles as well.  
A report title is not just decoration—it is closer to an entry point that tells the reader what flow they should follow.  
So I continued refining titles so the direction of each report comes through a little more clearly, rather than staying too generic or stiff.

I also worked on sign-up and login through Google accounts, along with welcome email handling.  
The basic groundwork is now in place to make the flow of entering the service feel a little more natural.

I adjusted Cron jobs as well.  
Because Market Memory relies on data that is generated and updated on a daily cycle,  
Cron is not a minor add-on—it is what shapes the rhythm of the service.  
At this stage, rather than perfect automation, what matters is building a structure that can run reliably on repeat.

## Designing SNS Posts for Marketing

Starting this week, I also began designing posts for marketing on SNS.

Market Memory reports  
ultimately need to reach someone,  
and that person needs to be able to sense “how this service looks at the market.”

So I am first putting together a structure for creating daily posts.

The direction I have in mind is not grand promotion.  
It is to create short posts for SNS based on the daily market summary and key flows that are already being generated.

Rather than pushing the service hard, it is closer to gradually showing how Market Memory views the market.

## Next Steps

Over this weekend and into early next week, I plan to finish the first-stage workflow for writing daily posts.

What matters at this stage is not perfect marketing automation.  
First, I want to confirm that posts in a form I can publish every day can be created reliably.

Next week, I plan to run a trial of the daily posting flow.  
By looking at the posts that are actually generated, I want to adjust tone, length, information density, CTA strength, and similar details.

Alongside that, I also want to try other kinds of posts.  
For example, weekly summaries, short explainers centered on a specific theme, or light introductions to features of the service.

I will continue Cron work as well.  
The main Cron task for next week will likely be running the multilingual (i18n) pipeline for original reports.

## Bringing Market Memory Out Into the World

Market Memory is now gradually moving away from being simply a “project to implement features.”

Of course, it is still at the MVP stage, and there is plenty to fix.  
But after deployment, looking at it with operations in mind,  
it feels like rhythm will matter more than the features themselves from here on.

Reading the market every day, organizing it, presenting it, and accumulating it again.  
And not ending that flow only inside the service, but also bringing it outward little by little through SNS and the blog.

Right now, I am in the stage of building that first operating rhythm.

By continuing to stack these small improvements and small records,  
I want to bring Market Memory out into the world—timidly, little by little, and slowly.
