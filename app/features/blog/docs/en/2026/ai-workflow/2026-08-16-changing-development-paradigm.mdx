Lately I often feel that the way I develop is changing little by little.

It is not only about **building with AI**.

Using AI is becoming less special and closer to a default.  
What feels larger is that developers are spending less time on every line of code,  
and more time shaping the overall frame, setting direction, and fitting the right pieces together.

At some point, I started working that way too.

## Letting Go of Implementation, Finding Direction

I used to care a lot about how complete my code felt.

A bit exaggerated, it was almost like a kind of perfectionism.

I kept checking whether classes were split well enough, whether there was duplication,  
whether each method had a clear role, and whether the structure could be a little cleaner.

In particular, I believed that **readable code should work like a detailed design document on its own**.

Even if I opened the code later without extra explanation, the structure and intent should still be clear.  
I also disliked leaving code I had not fully understood.

Of course, I do not think those standards are useless now.

I still ask AI whether something should be refactored,  
and when a structure feels too complex, I think about how to simplify it.

But the share of time and energy I put into that has clearly gone down.

What I do more often now is different.

I think about what to build, decide the direction of a feature, and choose how much structure it needs.

Then I pass that to AI.

A large part of the implementation goes to AI.

If the result is close enough to the intent and actually works, I often move on to the next step as is.

The old me might have looked at the code a little longer and only moved after I fully accepted it.

Now I care more about **how quickly an idea can take a real form**.

My attention is shifting from carving every piece myself to judging which pieces are needed.

## Computing Appears Like a Piece, Only When Needed

Recently I started studying Cloudflare Workers and Agent examples more seriously.

It has been much more fun than I expected.

What felt especially interesting was the way of looking at computing.

Traditionally, when I imagine a service, I first picture a central server,  
and that server's computing power handling requests.

Cloud environments like AWS have already made this structure quite flexible.

But the image I got while touching Cloudflare Workers was a little different.

**Somewhere in the world, computation wakes up when needed, only as much as needed, and disappears when its job is done.**

Leaving aside the exact internal architecture, as a developer looking at a service, that is close to what it feels like.

I like that image quite a bit.

Not keeping one huge fixed thing running all the time,  
but having the needed function appear at the needed moment.

In a way, computing itself feels like it is becoming a more fluid material.

Rather than stacking everything around one large server,  
it feels closer to connecting small pieces of capability into a single service.

## Screens Do Not Have to Be Finished Pieces in Advance

I heard something interesting in a similar vein.

Apparently Google is also exploring a direction where AI builds a screen in real time for each person, based on their situation and preferences.

Existing web pages are basically things people prepare in advance.

No matter who visits, the large structure of the page is already decided.

Even with personalization, what usually changes is the recommended content or data.

That might change a little going forward.

When one user opens a page, AI might assemble the UI itself in that moment, according to that person's goal and context.

One person may need a table, another a chart.

One person may need three menus, while another may not need those menus at all.

I do not think every service will suddenly work this way.

Cost would be high, and there seem to be many problems to solve around speed, stability, and consistent user experience.

But the direction itself is interesting.

And it strangely resembles the feeling I got from Cloudflare Workers.

**Rather than fixing everything in advance, make what is needed at the moment it is needed.**

In computing and in screens, a similar direction seems to be appearing.

## From Finishing Development Early to Assembling When Needed

Looking a little wider, coding with AI may also sit inside the same flow.

In the past, people wrote a lot of code ahead of time.

Going forward, people may set intent, rules, and structure,  
while AI produces a large share of the actual implementation as needed.

In environments like Cloudflare Workers, computation runs when it is needed.

And someday, user interfaces may also be composed more dynamically for each user's situation.

These are different technologies, but I feel a common thread.

**We are slowly moving from finishing fixed things in advance to assembling the needed pieces at the needed moment.**

Code, computing, and screens are all becoming a little more fluid as materials.

Of course, I do not think the old approach will disappear.

Central servers will still be needed,  
and UI designed and optimized by people will still matter.

In areas where stability and performance matter most, the existing approach will often still be better.

It feels more accurate to say that new approaches are not replacing everything that came before,  
but that the number of pieces we can assemble as needed keeps growing.

## Connecting and Verifying Matter More Than Making Every Piece

In that flow, the developer's role may also change little by little.

In the past, the ability to write the code itself was a fairly central strength for a developer.

Basic development knowledge will still matter, of course.

But if AI starts handling a large share of implementation,  
the abilities needed on top of that may shift a little.

Coming up with ideas,

breaking problems into the right size,

deciding which technologies and structures to use,

giving AI enough context and direction,

and connecting the resulting pieces into one system.

And I think one more thing becomes more important here.

**The eye that judges whether a piece was truly made well.**

Just because AI produces code quickly does not mean the result is always good.

It may look fine on the surface while the data model is off,  
or seem fine for now while being hard to extend later.

Even if two features are each well made,  
connecting them may tangle the boundaries of responsibility.

In the end, the more AI takes on implementation, the more developers may need  
**the ability to tell good from bad, and a sense for data modeling and architecture**.

Even without writing every line yourself,

you need to notice what feels wrong,  
judge which structures will last,  
and when needed, firmly ask for a rebuild.

The hand that writes code may matter less,  
while the eye that sees the whole may matter more.

## I Started Valuing Connection and Change Over Finish

So the standards I care about in development have also shifted a little.

I used to ask questions like these more often.

**Is this code clean enough?**

**Is this a structure I can accept?**

**Does the code alone read like a detailed design?**

Those standards still matter.

But lately I ask different questions more often.

**How quickly can this idea be implemented?**

**How easily can I check that it actually works?**

**Is this structure easy to validate?**

**If the direction is wrong, can I throw it away and rebuild without too much cost?**

**When a new idea appears, can I connect it without breaking the existing structure too much?**

In the end, more than making one perfect result from the start,  
what may matter more is building a structure that can keep replacing pieces, connecting them, and changing.

Seen that way, the developer's role also shifts little by little  
away from being the person who writes most of the code.

Someone who sets the overall direction,

defines the needed pieces,

uses AI and other tools to produce them,

judges whether they were made well,

and connects them into one system.

Lately I have started to see developers a little more in that light.

## Fitting Pieces Together Feels More Interesting Than Carving Them Well

I do not yet think this is some final answer.

AI is changing quickly,  
and the Cloudflare technologies I am studying now are still at the stage where I have only just started using them myself.

A few years from now, I may be thinking something entirely different.

Still, there is one change I clearly feel at this stage.

If I used to think a lot about **how well I could write the code**,

now I think more about **how quickly I can turn an idea into a real form, try it, and reassemble it**.

I am spending more time thinking about what to build than about writing the code itself.

Rather than carving every piece myself,  
I spend more time judging which pieces are needed  
and how to connect the pieces made by AI and existing technologies.

And checking whether those pieces truly fit together well  
also seems to be becoming a more important part of a developer's work.

Maybe this is a change that comes naturally from doing development for a long time,  
or maybe it is a change brought by AI as a tool.

Probably both.

I do not know yet how far this will go.

But lately I have started to see development as a much more fluid kind of work than before.

Not so much making one finished structure,  
but a structure you can keep making, connecting, verifying, and reassembling when needed.

That side is more interesting to me right now.
