## Crypto's Long Stagnation

While other assets kept breaking through new highs, the crypto market has spent a long stretch stuck in place.

Bitcoin rose to around $126,000 last October, then kept falling after that.

Compared with the previous high near $69,000 from November 2021, it did not even double — despite the long wait.

In February this year it came back down near $60,000, and even into early August the market still could not find a clear direction.

Still, Bitcoin has fared better than most.

The altcoin market was far worse.

Even Ethereum only barely cleared its previous high of 4.8K, touching around 4.9K before falling hard.  
It later dropped near 1.4K, and through early August spent a long stretch around 1.7K.

Looking only at the results, Ethereum's gains over the past five years amounted to almost nothing.

The broader crypto market looks similar.

Total market cap once reached around 4.4T, fell near 2T in February this year, and then lingered around that level for quite a while.

## I Think the Four-Year Cycle Has Already Broken

Many people in the market still put a lot of weight on Bitcoin's four-year cycle.

Based on past patterns, a bottom often formed about a year after a top.  
If last October was the top, then October or November this year could again look like candidates for an important low.

AI also treats past data and the market's existing consensus as important grounds for judgment.  
So when I asked AI, the reply also leaned toward a higher chance of an October low this year.

I take that seriously as well.  
These days, AI answers themselves shape people's decisions more than a little.

But I see it a little differently.

I do not think last October's Bitcoin high was the kind of top from the traditional four-year cycle we already know.

It feels closer to a high that once again burned the four-year cycle into people's minds — a cycle everyone had grown too used to.

And I think Bitcoin's four-year cycle itself has already broken.

The people in the market have changed, and so has the nature of the capital.

ETFs and institutional money have started to come in, and regulations and legal frameworks in many countries are being put in place quickly.

Too much has already changed for the whole market to keep repeating the same rhythm around a single halving, the way it did in the past.

So I do not attach much meaning to October this year as a date in itself.

I lean more toward the view that an important bottom already appeared during the decline so far.

At least for me, I think the area around a 2T total market cap will not break easily.

## This Is a Transition Toward Institutional Crypto

I do not think the current stagnation is only because the market lacks money.

It feels closer to a transition as crypto moves more fully into the institutional world.

Legal frameworks have to be rebuilt, standards set for investors and institutions to enter, and the industry as a whole has to prepare for that.

Discussions around market structure such as the Clarity Act are part of that process as well, in my view.

At the same time, there have been liquidity issues, and stretches when preference for risk assets was not as strong as before.

I think these factors have overlapped, and that is why a real start for Ethereum and the broader crypto market keeps getting delayed.

I cannot know how the market will move in the short term either.

Still, I carefully leave open the possibility that Ethereum strength may show up again.

## There May Still Be One Last Stretch of Alt Season

There is a scene that kept repeating in previous cycles.

Bitcoin rises first, then at some point Bitcoin dominance starts to break down, and money flows into Ethereum and altcoins.

I think that kind of move may show up again at least once.

It may even take a shape close to a final cleanup.

Crypto sentiment may improve again, alts may run freely with Ethereum at the center,  
and people may start telling optimistic stories again.

But I do not think it will look exactly like the past.

I also think Bitcoin dominance is unlikely to fall all the way to the levels everyone is hoping for.

Liquidity may start to open up, and everyone may expect an alt season like before —

**"That's it? Only this far?"**

— and it may end around that kind of point.

So even if an alt bubble returns,  
I see it less as a full new beginning, and a little more as the last large cycle before the old market structure finishes.

## Not Every Token Will Hold the Same Place Going Forward

Looking further out, I think the crypto market will be reshaped into something different from today.

Right now, thousands of coins are listed on exchanges worldwide, and most of them are traded as if they were the same kind of investment asset.

But I doubt that structure will hold forever.

I think tokens will gradually split by role.

Tokens listed on exchanges that attract capital from investors and institutions around the world.

Tokens that sit somewhere in between.

And utility tokens that serve real functions inside services or protocols.

In particular, listed tokens that attract large-scale capital are likely to face responsibilities closer and closer to those of stocks.

To freely take investment from people around the world, they will also need a matching level of preparation.

They will need to disclose information, follow certain rules, and earn trust from market participants.

Utility tokens, on the other hand, may move a little more freely as tools inside services rather than as investment assets.

In the end, I think only a small share of the thousands of coins that exist today will remain meaningful as investment assets.

Personally, I would not find it strange if only around 10% survived in that sense.

Of course, survival in the sense that a blockchain keeps running and a coin still trades on one or two exchanges could be more common.

What I mean by survival is a little different.

**Whether it remains an asset people still have a reason to put long-term capital into.**

## So the Next Rally Is Also a Time for Cleanup

Once I thought this through, what I need to do became a little clearer.

If a period comes when large liquidity enters the alt market one last time, I plan to cut the odds-and-ends coins I currently hold without hesitation.

I do not want to wait for break-even with too much hope, the way I used to.

Even if it means taking a loss, I plan to start by clearing more than 50%.

When the market gets better again, people's minds change quickly.

Maybe if I wait a little longer, it will go up.

Maybe this time really is different.

I'll sell once it gets back to the previous high.

It is easy to cling to hope again that way.

So I think it matters more to stick to principles decided in advance — especially when the market is good.

If the next rally comes, before asking what I should buy next,

**I need to decide first what I will no longer hold.**

## Compressing the Portfolio Again

After that, I want to fully reorganize my crypto portfolio itself.

The criteria are fairly simple.

**Bitcoin.**

I think of it as crypto itself.

And **Ethereum.**

To me, it is still the king of the crypto market.

Below that, instead of spreading across dozens of alts, I want to choose only about three or four assets that can represent each sector.

In the end, the next portfolio I have in mind looks roughly like this.

**Bitcoin — crypto itself**

**Ethereum — the king**

**Sector Leaders — about three or four assets representing each area (AI / RWA / Gaming, and so on)**

What matters is not holding many things, but keeping only assets I can explain why I hold.

And I want to keep studying those assets until the portfolio is simple enough that I can judge my own buy and sell criteria.

The king and the sector representatives can also change at any time.  
I am thinking of building an AI assistant agent to help analyze and judge those shifts as well.

## Crypto's Next Stage

I do not think the crypto market is coming to an end.

I think it is expanding into a form different from before.

But that expansion will not look like thousands of coins rising together, the way it did in the past.

First will come the process of entering the institutional world, separating roles,  
and distinguishing what can be recognized as an investment asset from what cannot.

Along the way, many tokens may disappear, and even those that survive may no longer receive the same valuation as before.

And if the market heats up again, that moment will be both a time for new expectations and a time to clean up the old portfolio.

I still cannot know what prices will look like from here.

But as of August 2026, I think the next crypto rally will not be a simple repeat of the last one.

## And Now

As of August 23, 2026, last week finally showed signs of ending the long sideways stretch.

Bitcoin started near 65K, touched $80,000, and is now around 76K.

Ethereum also rose from around 1.8K past 2.5K, and is now near 2.4K.

Total market cap went from around 2.15T to 2.68T, and is now near 2.58T.

Over the weekend, it looks like some of the overheating is cooling off.

After last week's rise, the case for a 2T total-market floor looks even stronger.

Of course, how things unfold still needs more watching.

Personally, I find myself hoping for a half-baked bubble that catches most people — including AI, and those who support the October-bottom thesis — a little off guard.
