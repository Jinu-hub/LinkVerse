# Spending the Time Left Happily

Less than two months of 2026 remain.

Time has gone by so quickly. Still, looking back on this year, quite a lot seems to have changed inside me.

Work, personal projects, relationships, and even the way I look at ordinary days.

It was not one particular event that changed me. Rather, many experiences and thoughts seem to have built on one another, changing me little by little.

My perspective has broadened a little, and I feel more at ease.

Above all, I have started to understand what gratitude and abundance mean, and to welcome them into my life.

I do not stay in that frame of mind all the time.

I still get irritated over small things, and I still feel rushed. When things do not work out the way I hoped, I still get stressed.

But at least how I want to live is clearer than it used to be.

Maybe that is the biggest change of this year.

## How I Live, More Than What I Achieve

For a long time, I spent much of my time working toward something.

Growing as a developer, building a financial foundation, creating my own services, and looking for new possibilities.

I still think those goals matter. That has not changed.

These days, though, I have also started to care about the state of mind I carry through the process.

I do not want to keep postponing today's happiness just to reach a goal.

I want to do the work I enjoy while still having room to breathe, spend good time with people, and look after my health.

A life where an ordinary day can still feel like enough.

I used to think I would feel abundant if I had more of what I wanted. Now I think I can also feel abundance by recognizing what I already have.

That does not mean I am giving up my ambitions or goals.

There is still a lot I want to achieve, and a lot I want to try.

I just want to go after all of it with a little more joy.

## What I Want to Do in the Two Months Left

These thoughts naturally led me to consider how I want to spend the rest of the year.

Nothing grand.

Just small goals for making the most of my everyday life.

### Balancing My Current Work with New Opportunities

At work, I want to carefully work out my role in the AI-related projects.

It looks like I will keep my current work, and also take part in some of the AI-related projects.

I am interested in the field, and it is one where I can use the experience I have built up so far, so part of me is looking forward to it.

Still, taking on new work is not necessarily all positive.

I have to think about balance with the work I already have. What the workplace expects of the role, and what I expect, may not be the same.

What matters is finding a direction that helps both the workplace and me.

If we can understand each other's position and find a reasonable balance, I think it will become a good experience.

### Making Room for Exercise Again

There is something I have let slide lately.

Exercise. haha

For a while I had been fairly consistent, but recently I have been working out much less often.

Being busy was one reason. Honestly, though, there were many days when I could have made the time if I had just decided to.

For the rest of the year, I want to get the habit back.

Not by setting an overly ambitious goal, but by moving a little and keeping at it.

Nothing can really replace good health, so I need to stop letting it slide down the list.

### A Drive, and Time with People

Before the year ends, I want to rent a car at least once and go for a drive.

It is fine if there is no particular place to get to.

Driving with music I like, and pulling over for a while when I find a view I enjoy.

Sometimes I need a small break from the usual like that.

Lately I have also started showing up again at gatherings I had drifted away from.

I have always liked being with people, and I enjoy the time spent talking and laughing together.

For a while, I had fewer opportunities to do that. Going back to these gatherings recently has reminded me how much I enjoy them.

Before the year ends, I would like to attend about two more gatherings—perhaps four, if time allows.

Time alone is good. Time with people I like is also a real part of what makes my days feel abundant.

I went to Busan in October, and it looks like I will go back briefly in early December.

I am looking forward to spending another enjoyable few days in Korea before the year is over.

### Keep the Personal Projects Going, Without Pushing

I plan to keep building personal projects too.

This year, AI-assisted development evolved rapidly, and I learned and experienced a great deal along the way.

Learning new technology, building services myself, and seeing what was possible—the process was genuinely enjoyable.

At the same time, the things I wanted to do kept piling up, and I did make myself a little too busy.

So lately I have been slowing down on purpose.

I do not want a personal project to stop being something I enjoy and turn into an obligation.

It is fine if things take a little longer.

What matters is continuing to build without losing sight of the other parts of daily life I care about.

I want to keep making things, steadily, without pushing too hard.

## In the End, One Goal Matters Most

Written out like this, there is nothing particularly grand about the plan.

Sorting out work, exercising, going for a drive, seeing people, visiting Korea, and keeping the personal projects moving a little at a time.

In a way, these are very ordinary things.

Right now, though, ordinary days like these feel quite precious to me.

And the biggest goal for the rest of the year is actually something else.

**To close out 2026 truly happily.**

By happiness, I do not mean everything going to plan or getting every result I want.

Something unexpected may come up. I may not follow through on what I planned.

That is fine.

I want to accept those moments as part of my life too, and meet them with a little more ease.

I want to be grateful for what I have, fully enjoy the good moments when they come, and not carry difficult experiences in my heart for too long.

It will not be as easy as saying it. Still, I want to live toward that.

On the last day of the year, I hope I can look back for a moment and think this:

"I lived this year well."

It would be even better if I remember how happily I lived, rather than how much I accomplished.

There is still a little time before the year ends.

I do not know what awaits me in the time left. Right now, though, I am looking forward to it.

**Without rushing. Without stopping, either.**

Let's make the end of 2026 a happy one.
